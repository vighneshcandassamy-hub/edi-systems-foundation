# Budworm story: a substrate and discovery challenge

Date: 2026-10-19. Status: conceptual challenge supplied by the owner; no experiment run. This does not replace the six-function architecture or add a domain-specific ecological ontology.

## Verdict and source boundary

EDI can plausibly be developed to investigate a system of this kind. Its present design and historical tests do not demonstrate that discovery. No finite observer can guarantee a complete model of every real-world structure or nonlinearity. A defensible target is a revisable model sufficient for declared questions, which can expose missing influences, expand boundaries and abstain where evidence does not distinguish mechanisms.

The owner supplied the Meadows passage. It describes interacting forest composition, insect reproduction, natural enemies, climate, management and industry incentives. Receiving that passage already supplies a causal interpretation. Extracting it accurately tests comprehension and representation, not independent discovery of its mechanisms.

Primary cross-check: Ludwig, D., Jones, D. D. and Holling, C. S. (1978), Qualitative Analysis of Insect Outbreak Systems: The Spruce Budworm and Forest, Journal of Animal Ecology 47, 315–332, DOI 10.2307/3939. [Research paper](https://www.math.utah.edu/~keener/classes/math5110/papers/ludwig.pdf), retrieved 2026-10-19. The paper's summary separates fast and slow variables and describes possible relaxation oscillations using qualitative information, with parameter information adding insight. This supports a modeling approach, not EDI's capability or the completeness of the historical explanation. Historical quantities and every ecological claim in the supplied story have not been independently re-audited here. No paper/book copy is committed.

## What the challenge demands

| Feature of the supplied account | What EDI would need to do | Design consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A warm spring coincides with an outbreak | Separate a possible trigger from the accumulated conditions that made it effective | Preserve temporal context, alternative explanations and long histories. |
| Slow forest succession interacts with rapid insect change | Compare multiple temporal scales and relevant latent/aggregate state | Do not require one time step, window or fixed boundary. |
| Relationships change with population and food availability | Infer conditional nonlinear response rather than a universal constant-strength edge | Host versioned response rules and conditional evidence. |
| Predator response ceases to keep pace | Investigate response limits and thresholds | A larger prey count need not imply proportional effective control. |
| Outbreaks remove food and alter forest composition | Represent delayed balancing loops and changing dominant mechanisms | Model loop interaction and regime change, not just pairwise correlation. |
| Spraying suppresses immediate insects but alters future conditions | Compare direct and indirect effects under management selection | Keep action/settings/outcome history and avoid treating selected observations as random samples. |
| Ecological persistence conflicts with steady industrial supply | Separate dynamics from stakeholder objectives and constraints | EDI cannot infer what should be optimized from observations alone. |
| Bounded oscillation can persist | Recognize cycles and multiple regimes as possible normal behavior | Do not equate natural intelligence or system health with convergence to one steady state. |

## A plausible investigation, not an observed EDI result

Start from time/location/source-linked observations: insect density and defoliation, tree species/age/foliage, predator/pathogen observations, weather, spraying settings, harvest and economic decisions, plus sampling effort and unknown coverage. Preserve native imagery, field notes, counts and tree-ring evidence with uncertainty and provenance. Tree-ring damage alone cannot uniquely identify all predator or management mechanisms.

Build competing partial explanations: weather trigger only; food-dependent growth; limited natural control; combined feedback; reporting/sampling changes; or another unobserved mechanism. Do not seed the final ecological equations as supposedly discovered structure. An LLM's suggested predator mechanism is imported prior knowledge until discriminated by observations.

Record predictions that differ: for example, does a weather-only model predict equally large outbreaks in low- and high-foliage stands? Do comparable stands with differing management histories exhibit different natural-control dynamics? What changes before an outbreak, and over what lag? These are proposed observational questions; confounding can prevent causal conclusions. Prefer retained evidence and ethically permitted observations before seeking interventions. Acquisition requests do not authorize spraying or withholding control.

Use actual additional evidence to revise which explanation remains viable. If two mechanisms predict the same available observations, preserve both and identify the missing measurement or permitted test. Unobserved relationships may exist; they are not automatically identifiable. Conversely, lack of a useful unknown relationship can justify stopping rather than inventing one.

The strategic output could warn, conditionally and with evidence, that short-term suppression may preserve conditions for persistent dependence on suppression. It should explain alternatives, timing, uncertainty and whose objective is being served. It must not automatically recommend ending treatment: different management actions and human/ecological tradeoffs require their own evidence and authority.

## What would count as a demonstration

1. Story comprehension: reconstruct the supplied account without claiming independent discovery.
2. Mechanism discovery in a controlled unfamiliar system: withhold the story and causal labels; expose partial streams; check proposal formation, useful observation requests and prospective regime/threshold predictions.
3. Discrimination and revision: supply new evidence that separates rivals or contradicts the initial explanation. Preserve correct uncertainty, update dependencies and avoid self-confirming model output.
4. Null and mismatch cases: include systems without saturation, without cycles and with indistinguishable causes. EDI must not hallucinate the familiar story everywhere.
5. Broader evidence: evaluate across altered parameters, sampling regimes and structures, then independently justified real evidence. A simulator test is limited by the simulator's assumptions.

Freeze protocol, baselines, metrics, model access, fresh evidence and full resource accounting before running a comparison. Source story/paper exposure contaminates this specific case as a blind novelty test for a pretrained model; unfamiliar variants and causal controls remain necessary. Current status for every proposed demonstration: NOT RUN.

## Architectural implication

The [blueprint](SUBSTRATE_BLUEPRINT.md) must make this exploration expressible and revisable. That requires native evidence, conditional dynamics, nested/overlapping boundaries, multi-timescale history, alternatives, intervention/selection lineage and stakeholder goals kept separate. Hosting these concerns is necessary but insufficient: proposal/search rules, model identification and discriminating inquiry must actually be developed and tested. Those remain explicit open choices rather than a claim that the substrate already covers the whole world.
